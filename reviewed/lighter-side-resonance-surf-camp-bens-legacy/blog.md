# Ben's Legacy: How the Resonance Surf Camp is Redefining Inclusion and Communication

In this special episode of *The Lighter Side of the Spectrum*, hosts David Koffer and Kim Kredich reflect on a transformative three-day inclusive surf camp in Wrightsville Beach. They explore how the "Resonance Surf Camp" honors the memory of their late son, Ben, while demonstrating that true inclusion requires tailored support, shared community, and innovative communication methods. This post details the camp's evolution, the profound impact of spelling boards on personal growth, and the ripple effects of creating belonging for families navigating neurodiversity.

## The Journey from IndoJack to Resonance

The story of the Resonance Surf Camp began not as a formal charity event, but as a personal habit for Kim Kredich and her family. After her son was diagnosed with autism in 2001, Kim and her husband began attending IndoJack Surf School with their three sons. Over several years, the family's commitment to the surf school evolved into a broader mission. Kim invited other families she had advocated for—children of all abilities and their siblings—to join them, transforming the experience into a truly inclusive environment.

This initiative took shape during the 2016, 2017, and 2018 seasons. However, the camp's identity was forever changed in August 2023, when their son Ben was tragically killed by an impaired driver. In the wake of this loss, the community rallied, with many donating to the IndoJack Surf Charity in Ben's memory. These contributions helped fund a subsequent season of the camp, which was officially renamed the Resonance Surf Camp to honor Ben's spirit and legacy.

## A Partnership with the Peyton Project

The evolution of the camp was solidified through a strategic partnership with the Peyton Project, a Tennessee-based foundation dedicated to promoting communication, specifically for those using letterboards and spelling to express themselves. Recognizing the unique value of the camp's approach to inclusion and brain-body connection, the Peyton Project stepped in to support the initiative.

The foundation agreed to take over the camp's operations, ensuring its longevity and reach. This transition allowed the camp to continue its mission of connecting families with the ocean while providing the necessary supports for diverse needs. The collaboration highlighted how external resources can amplify the work of families who are already driving meaningful change in their communities.

## The Power of Spelling and Communication

A central theme of the episode is the transformative power of communication tools, particularly spelling boards, in fostering independence and self-expression. Kim Kredich shared a poignant anecdote about a participant named Cole, who initially struggled to express his desire to participate in an activity. Through the use of a spelling board, Cole was able to spell out, "I want to do this," a moment that underscored the importance of listening and supporting the heart and soul of the individual.

Kim noted that the camp opened her mind further, teaching her that when a person's communication needs are met, their potential is fully realized. She observed how spelling has given a "gift" to everyone involved, breaking down barriers that often prevent participation. The camp serves as a living example of how accommodating modifications and support systems can lead to genuine inclusion, mirroring successful models in educational settings.

## Standing Starts and Breaking Motor Loops

The surf camp offered more than just waves; it provided a space to observe and understand the unique challenges neurodivergent individuals face, such as motor loops or repetitive movements. Kim Kredich described watching a participant named Peyton on the surfboard, who repeatedly stood up in a loop rather than progressing to a more stable stance. This behavior reminded Kim of a photo she had taken of Peyton in 2020, where he was kneeling on the board.

Realizing that Peyton was in a "motor loop" and clearly wanted to surf, Kim suggested a new approach: starting him on his knees to gradually move toward standing. This adjustment led to the creation of a "standing start" technique for "spelling surfers." This innovation demonstrated how understanding the specific mechanics of a child's movement can lead to successful inclusion. It reinforced the idea that support must be tailored to the individual's current state to set them up for success.

## Community Belonging and Shared Experiences

Beyond the water, the camp emphasized the importance of community building through shared experiences. David Koffer highlighted the unprecedented level of support from instructors and families, noting that every surfer had at least one dedicated instructor with them. The camp moved beyond the beach to include communal dinners, such as pizza and taco nights, held at various locations, including an Airbnb hosted by Karen and Chad.

These gatherings created a sense of belonging that Kim Kredich described as "magical." Unlike previous iterations where families might stay in separate hotels or groups, this model brought everyone together.
