When the body will not do what the brain is asking

This week on The Lighter Side of the Spectrum, David talks with Dr. Antigone Oreopoulos, a physiotherapist and mom in Edmonton whose son Stellan spells on a letterboard.

The conversation starts from a simple problem. A person can understand the request, and still be unable to make the body carry it out. That gap gets labeled refusal, noncompliance, or a lack of understanding. Antigone argues it is often a sensory-motor and motor-planning problem, and that the research has been saying so for years.

She reads two young spellers, Peyton Thorpe and Marcus Hulette, on what that feels like: a brain watching from the stands while the body spins, daydreams, or knocks over the glass it meant to pick up. She and David talk about ABA score sheets that punish a missed movement, IQ tests that measure pointing instead of thinking, and why walking across a room is not proof that motor support is unnecessary.

The practical pieces are concrete. Screen the early motor milestones. Give more breaks than you think are needed. Do the skill yourself before you teach it. And if speech and movement do not match, verify with the letterboard. Words are the reliable signal. The body may be doing the opposite.
