# Autism, Trauma, and Advocacy: A Father's Journey with Stephen Laine

In this episode of *The Lighter Side of the Spectrum*, host David Kaufer sits down with Stephen Laine to explore the often-overlooked intersection of autism and trauma. Drawing from his personal experience as a father, advocate, and writer, Stephen shares the raw reality of navigating the healthcare system, managing divorce, and supporting two sons with autism through significant life transitions. This conversation highlights the importance of recognizing emotional wounds in autistic children and the vital role of honest advocacy in creating better outcomes.

## The Intersection of Autism and Trauma

Many people associate autism primarily with communication differences, sensory challenges, or repetitive behaviors, often missing the profound impact of trauma. Stephen Laine emphasizes that while trauma is not an inherent part of the autism spectrum, it is a frequent companion for autistic children and adults. Repeated negative experiences—such as school struggles, bullying, hospitalizations, and family disruption—leave lasting emotional wounds that change how individuals interact with the world.

Stephen's journey illustrates this connection vividly. His sons experienced significant trauma not only from their diagnoses but also from major life upheavals, including divorce and school district changes. These events created confusion and distress, particularly because autistic children often struggle to understand complex concepts like separation or the dissolution of a family unit.

*   **The Reality of Trauma:** Trauma often stems from a world not designed for neurodivergent individuals, leading to feelings of misunderstanding and exclusion.
*   **Emotional Impact:** Events like hospitalizations and elopement (running away) can cause deep emotional damage that requires specific support.
*   **The Link:** As Stephen notes, autism care and trauma "go hand in hand," requiring a holistic approach to healing.

## Diagnosing and Navigating the System

The path to understanding autism can be difficult, especially when initial signs are subtle or misunderstood. Stephen recounts the journey of diagnosing his two sons, Matthew and Aiden, in 2011. Matthew was three and a half years old and was not hitting developmental milestones, communicating only with hums. Despite initial skepticism from the school district, two medical opinions confirmed an autism diagnosis.

Similarly, Aiden was diagnosed after showing signs of stacking blocks with perfect alignment and humming, a behavior Stephen recognized as indicative of autism. However, the journey did not end with the diagnosis; it began with the challenge of accessing care. At the time, ABA (Applied Behavior Analysis) services were not covered by insurance in Michigan, costing approximately $30,000 per year out of pocket.

*   **Early Signs:** Delays in walking, communication, and social interaction often prompt early evaluation.
*   **Systemic Barriers:** Lack of insurance coverage for therapies like ABA can make treatment financially inaccessible for many families.
*   **Alternative Pathways:** When standard therapies are unavailable, parents must find creative, often limited, ways to provide support.

## The Impact of Divorce and Transition

Divorce presents a unique set of challenges for families with autistic children. Stephen and his wife separated in September 2019, a transition that caused significant trauma for his sons. Unlike neurotypical children who might understand the concept of divorce, autistic children often struggle to process the sudden change in living arrangements and the loss of their mother's presence.

The boys faced confusion about why they were no longer living together and the loss of their belongings. Stephen had to take full-time custody, moving the boys from Oxford, Michigan, to suburban Detroit. This move was traumatic, leading to meltdowns and behavioral regressions, particularly for Aiden, who struggled to cope with the changes in his school environment.

*   **Confusion and Grief:** Autistic children may struggle to understand the concept of divorce, leading to questions like "Why are we not together anymore?"
*   **Loss of Belongings:** The transition involved losing all personal belongings, which was a significant source of distress.
*   **Behavioral Changes:** The stress of the transition resulted in hyperactivity, lack of focus, and meltdowns in the classroom.

## The Role of Advocacy and Support

As a single father managing his sons' lives full-time, Stephen realized that advocacy was not a choice but a necessity. He described the moment he decided to step up, stating, "I didn't choose advocacy; advocacy chose me." This realization came when he understood that if he did not fight for his children's needs, no one else would.

His advocacy work involved fighting for appropriate services in schools and challenging methods that were not working for his sons. While he maintained good relationships with school administrations, there were instances where he had to intervene directly to ensure his sons received the support they needed to thrive.

*   **Personal Responsibility:** Stephen believes that parents must take ownership of their children's advocacy to ensure their well-being.
*   **School Collaboration:** Building strong relationships with school staff is crucial for navigating educational needs.
*   **Community Support:** Stephen relies heavily on his mother's help, highlighting the importance of family networks in providing care.
