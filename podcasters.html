Why my transcripts are different

  • Human-reviewed, not blindly AI-generated. I read the output and correct errors — names, terminology and sense.
  • Speaker identification. Multiple voices are separated and labelled, not left as an undifferentiated wall of text.
  • Context-aware cleanup. Filler and false starts are handled the way you'd want for your audience.
  • Technical terminology. Niche and specialist terms are checked, not guessed.
  • Timestamp accuracy. Speaker-change timestamps by default, with interval options if you prefer.
  • Unclear audio is flagged. If a passage is genuinely inaudible, I mark it — I never invent words.
  • Final proofreading. Every deliverable is checked before it reaches you.
  • One revision included. Tweaks after delivery are part of the service.

Why not just use AI?

Whisper, Otter and Descript are good — and I use AI transcription too. The difference is what happens next. An automated pass gets you close; a human review gets you publishable.

AI aloneAI + human review (this service)
Misheard names, brands and technical terms left uncorrectedNames, terminology and figures verified against the context
Speakers often merged into one undifferentiated blockVoices separated and labelled so quotes are attributable
No judgement on filler, false starts or inaudible passagesCleaned to read well; genuinely inaudible parts flagged, never invented
You still format, caption, chapter and publish it yourselfDelivered as transcript, captions, chapters and a ready web page
Good enough to skimGood enough to publish, quote and repurpose

Accessibility isn't an add-on

Transcripts and captions give people another way to access spoken content — whether they are Deaf or hard of hearing, process information better through text, want to search for a specific passage, or simply can't listen at the moment. My workflow produces both a readable transcript and SRT/VTT captions so your episodes are easier for more people to use.

A note on scope: captions and a transcript are important steps, but they aren't a complete accessibility solution on their own. If you're working toward broader accessibility — for example audio description or a fully accessible page — I'm glad to talk through what fits your situation.

Frequently asked questions

How are rates calculated?

Pricing is based on the length of your audio or video in minutes, with a small adjustment per additional speaker (more speakers means more diarization and review time). Bulk and recurring episodes are discounted — contact me for a tailored quote.

What about multiple speakers?

Separating and labeling speakers accurately takes extra care, so each additional speaker adds a modest premium. Up to two speakers are included in the base rate; further speakers are priced per speaker.

What is your workflow?

1) You send a file or link. 2) I transcribe and diarize the speakers. 3) I review for accuracy and formatting. 4) You receive SRT, VTT, TXT and DOCX (plus an optional analytics report). 5) One round of free revisions.

What are your turnaround times?

Standard delivery is within a few business days; rush (24–48h) is available for an added fee. Longer or lower-quality audio may take a little longer, and I'll confirm a date up front.

What features are included?

Timestamps, speaker labels, SRT/VTT subtitles, clean and verbatim text versions, a searchable HTML transcript, and an analytics report (talk-time share, speaking pace, filler-word count and keyword highlights).

What kind of audio can you handle?

Unclear, noisy or phone-recorded audio, strong accents, technical or niche terminology, multiple overlapping speakers, and custom timestamp or speaker requirements. If a section is genuinely inaudible I'll flag it rather than guess.

What don't you do?

I don't fabricate words for inaudible passages, don't provide translation unless arranged in advance, don't share your files, and don't promise 100% verbatim perfection on heavily degraded audio — but I'll get as close as possible and mark any uncertainties.

What service packages do you offer?

I use a simple flat-rate model rather than fixed tiers: you pay a per-minute base rate for the length of your audio or video, plus a small adjustment per speaker for diarization and review. Add-ons — show notes, timestamp chapters, a blog post, an analytics report and shareable quote graphics — are mixed and matched to your needs and billed on top of the base. Bulk and recurring episodes get a discount. Use the price calculator for an instant quote.

Can you work directly from YouTube?

Yes. You can send me a YouTube link, an audio/video file, or another accessible source. I'll confirm the source and delivery requirements before starting.

Can you preserve names and specialist terminology?

Yes. Every transcript is human-reviewed: I check names, specialist and niche terminology, and correct the errors an automated pass misses. If a passage is genuinely unclear, I flag it rather than guess — so the people and ideas in your episode come through accurately.
